Research

My research lies in corpus pragmatics and digital linguistics as well as in the discourse analysis and sociolinguistics of contemporary German. I work empirically, with digital methods and from a cultural-analytic perspective.

Discourse, knowledge, society

I am interested in how language brings forth knowledge and culture. I start from a constructivist concept of language grounded in cultural studies and follow the thesis that what counts as fact in a society, who appears as actor and audience and which norms apply is produced in language use and can therefore also be observed in it. Accordingly, I understand facticity, subjectivity and normativity as semiotically and linguistically emergent phenomena that materialise in communicative artefacts such as media texts, web forum discussions, everyday conversation or the various media of protest.

In my dissertation I used the example of HIV|AIDS in German-speaking Switzerland to reconstruct how the meaning of this discourse object has changed since the infection became treatable and public interest in it has faded. From the finding that older knowledge runs along in current patterns of language use without being explicitly asserted, I derived the concept of discourse latency. The monograph is under review. Further work concerns Wikipedia, conspiracy theories, 1980s protest communication and the linguistic construction of sexuality.

Postdigital writing cultures

Against a posthumanist and postdigital background, I ask how writing, and texts as its products, can be thought about in times of LLMs. I understand writing as an assemblage of human and non-human participants, of tools, epistemes and norms, from whose interplay a product emerges. I call writing cultures postdigital when digital tools and generative AI are no longer the exception but an unmarked part of the writing context. I read the growing spread of large language models not primarily as a disruptive force but as a catalyst of tendencies already present in digital writing.

In my habilitation project I study these shifts in school and academic writing, along the ideologies, practices and products that make up a writing culture. Part of the data comes from participatory projects with schools in which students and teachers take part as co-researchers. Postdigital texts also raise questions of their own, such as interactivity, multimodality and patterns of AI-generatedness. I pursue such questions in my projects on chatbots in social administration and on multimodal corpus pragmatics as well.

Corpus pragmatics and digital methods

While the first two areas name fields of research, this one is about method and methodology. For me, digital methods are not tools that one applies to data. They help bring forth the data they analyse: what becomes visible as a pattern depends on how a procedure models language use. Methodologically I follow the position of data philology. In this understanding, computing, reading and visualising interlock, and the centre is no longer occupied by the classical text alone but by various forms of data: the text stands alongside lists and visualisations as one of several units to be interpreted. In addition, every procedure has to be questioned for the research logic it brings into the analysis. Only those who can rebuild a procedure know which results are due to the object and which to the tool.

I developed this position in my dissertation and pursued it further in the project Research logics in text-based digital humanities. It has led to my own implementations and to an open toolbox for analysis and visualisation. I am currently extending corpus pragmatics to multimodal and interaction-based communication. I treat large language models like any other procedure: as a tool that needs testing. This is linked to a question in language theory: to what extent is a corpus pragmatics that reconstructs meaning from patterns of use epistemically comparable to how language models proceed?

Ongoing

  • since 2024
    Postdigital writing cultures (habilitation project)

    In my habilitation project I study how generative AI changes writing practices, texts and ideas about writing in postdigital environments, above all in school and academic writing. Three sub-studies address writing ideologies in guidelines, teaching material and online discourse, practices in screen recordings and writing process data, and products through text and corpus linguistic analysis. The aim is a theory of postdigital writing cultures as a cultural-linguistic contribution to the debate on AI literacy.

    Habilitation project | University of Zurich

  • since 2026
    Language Regimes of the Machine: Language Ideologies and Multilingual Practice under Generative AI (with Naomi Truan, Leiden)

    Generative AI counts as multilingual, yet it treats languages like a menu of separate, internally uniform units. The project puts speakers at the centre: what do multilingual people do with these systems, what do the systems do to their practices, and do the concepts of sociolinguistics still hold when one of the "persons" involved is a machine? Its first activity is the international workshop Doing Multilingualism with Generative AI: Recentering the Users on 10 and 11 March 2027 in Zurich, with keynotes by Bettina Migge (Dublin) and Anna Weichselbraun (Vienna).

    Led with Naomi Truan (Leiden University) | University of Zurich Global Funding Scheme

  • 2026–2028
    SchrAIb-Tutor: fostering academic writing competence in times of AI (principal investigator)

    A modular, openly accessible self-learning environment accompanies students along the six phases of the academic writing process, from orientation to editing, and addresses writing practices such as excerpting, paraphrasing, citing and arguing. Generative AI is considered in every phase: what can a tool do, where are its limits, and which decisions remain with the writer?

    Principal investigator, with Elisabeth Zima | University Teaching Fund UZH (open_innovation)

  • 2026–2027
    Chatbots at the interface between social administration and its public (expert report)

    Social administrations such as job centres increasingly use chatbots in their contact with citizens. The expert report asks how the resulting chat logs can be obtained and made usable for research, and develops a best-practice guideline together with a prototype sketch. The Zurich part covers corpus building, preparation and documentation including legal and research-ethical questions, as well as the workshop demonstrator.

    Expert report commissioned by the Institute for Work, Skills and Training, University of Duisburg-Essen | led by Bettina M. Bock (Cologne), Zurich part with Xenia Bojarski

  • since 2026
    Multimodal corpus pragmatics: the youth magazine Bravo as a test case (methods project, IMPRS)

    Corpus pragmatics is text-oriented and largely ignores layout and image. Using a multimodal corpus of Bravo (1956–1999), I examine how text, image and layout can be processed so that text-image relations become analysable corpus-pragmatically, and what language and vision-language models contribute compared with manual work, in segmentation and annotation as well as in the analysis of the data itself.

    Methods project | International Max Planck Research School for Multimodal Digital Humanities (Bibliotheca Hertziana, Rome)

  • 2025–2027
    AI in teaching: promising practices in the humanities and social sciences (project coordination)

    The project examines how generative AI can be used meaningfully in humanities and social science teaching, or deliberately not used. Instead of rigid best practices it focuses on promising practices: approaches adapted to study programmes, modules and individual courses and developed further as the technology advances. The aim is a toolkit for teaching, learning and assessment processes at the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences.

    Project coordination | led by Sandro Zanetti and Peter Finke | University Teaching Fund UZH

Completed

  • 2024–2025
    Writing with AI? I can! (citizen science project)

    Citizen science project in which upper-secondary students co-researched their own writing practices with generative AI: they collected data, analysed screen recordings and discussed the results. The material feeds into the habilitation project.

    Principal investigator | Citizen Science Zurich (ETH Zurich and University of Zurich)

  • 2018–2020
    Research logics in text-based digital humanities (coordination)

    The project examined which ideas of language and text are inscribed in the tools of text-based digital humanities and how they shape the findings.

    Coordination | SNSF project within the Digital Lives programme, led by Noah Bubenhofer

  • 2015–2023
    Communication and (non-)knowledge about HIV/AIDS (doctoral project)

    Doctoral project on the linguistic conceptualisation of HIV/AIDS in German-speaking Switzerland in media and web forums, from which the concept of discourse latency emerged.

    Doctoral project | Doc.CH fellowship of the Swiss National Science Foundation and Research Fund of the University of Zurich

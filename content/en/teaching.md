## Teaching philosophy

In my courses, students work with data and sources and have the opportunity to get hands-on experience of the different phases of linguistic research. I do not treat methods as neutral: whoever understands a procedure knows which findings are due to the object and which to the tool. This applies to corpus tools as much as to generative AI.

What I aim at is more than subject knowledge: a thought style in Ludwik Fleck's sense that understands language as social practice and enables students to analyse linguistic phenomena independently, on an empirical basis and critically. I align learning objectives, learning activities and assessments with one another and make them transparent from the start. I treat writing conventions and examination logics as learnable practices that are accompanied step by step, with a first draft, peer feedback modelled on academic peer review, and revision. This also includes communicating research to a specific audience, for instance when students present their posters to a school class.

With Noah Bubenhofer I have designed and coordinated the summer school *Zürcher Korpuslinguistik und Korpuspragmatik* (ZuKoKo) since 2021. I also give regular training courses for teachers, mainly on the Matura thesis and on writing with AI, and have co-developed several digital learning environments, among them *KoDuP*, an introduction to corpus analysis with Python for students of German.

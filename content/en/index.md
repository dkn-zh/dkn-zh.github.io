I am a linguist and German studies scholar at the Department of German Studies, University of Zurich. Two questions drive my research: (1) How are knowledge, identity and communicative norms negotiated in language use, and what does this tell us about a digitally shaped society? And (2) how should we think about language when platforms, tools and generative systems increasingly take part in producing it alongside humans? I pursued the first question in my dissertation, using the example of how HIV|AIDS is given meaning in the media and in web forums. I pursue the second in my habilitation project on postdigital writing cultures, with a focus on writing with language models.

I understand language in **usage-based and cultural-analytic** terms as a practice in which knowledge and meaning are produced and emerge and solidify as patterns of language use. Digitality plays a double role here: It changes the objects of linguistics, from web forums and platform communication to interaction with language models. And it changes the procedures we use to study these objects.

In method, computing and reading intertwine in my work in the spirit of a **data philology**. No qualitative analysis of patterning does without quantitative assumptions, and no quantitative analysis without qualitative interpretation. Neither concepts nor digital tools are neutral in this, but follow their own **research logics**, which help determine what counts as a finding. I therefore make these research logics an object of study themselves and ask which assumptions about language they carry and what they render visible or also obscure in the material.

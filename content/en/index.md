Thinking language and digitality together is the core of my research programme, in method and in subject. My research lies in **corpus pragmatics and digital linguistics** as well as in the **cultural, discourse and sociolinguistics** of contemporary German. Semantics and pragmatics are its foundations. I work from a cultural-analytic perspective, empirically and with digital methods. Digitality plays a double role here. It changes the **objects** of linguistics, from web forums and platform communication to interaction with language models. And it changes the **procedures** we use to study them.

My concept of language is **usage-based and cultural-analytic**. Language is a practice in which knowledge and meaning are produced and solidify into patterns of use. Two guiding questions follow. What can contemporary German tell us about how a digitally shaped society orders knowledge, marks belonging and shifts communicative norms? And how should language be described when platforms, tools and generative systems increasingly take part in producing it alongside humans? In my work, **computing and reading** intertwine. I examine every tool for the research logic it brings into the analysis.
